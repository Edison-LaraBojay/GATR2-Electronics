  - W1's own `channels.state.last_latency_ms` said ~31 ms. The rest is most likely bytes already written ahead of the newest state: the in-flight frame (up to a whole ~21 KB diag, about 0.2 s at this rate) plus the kernel socket buffers. 0.9 s at 100 kB/s is about 90 KB. Inferred, not traced.
  - The slow client's bytes were mostly diag (61 kB/s of 100) and previews (24 kB/s); state was 4 kB/s.

## Risks
- **Shared host.** Other workers and the user's apps loaded the host: 23% to 100% of all cores busy during scenarios, recorded per scenario. Absolute numbers move with load; use interleaved A/B for before/after.
- **Browser setup.** Headless Chrome here uses the real GPU (ANGLE D3D11, RTX 4060) at about 120 Hz rAF. A 60 Hz laptop halves the per-second render work.
- **Network throttling.** Chrome's CDP network throttling did not slow the WebSocket. Slow links are covered by the C++ slow client only.
- **Viewer in a Worker.** If the final viewer moved its socket into a Worker, the probe would no longer see onmessage cost (the live viewer uses `ws.onmessage` on the main thread, so it is fine today).
